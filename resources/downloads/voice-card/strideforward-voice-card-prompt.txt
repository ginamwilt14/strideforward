Please help me make a voice card: one page that tells any AI assistant how I sound, so the drafts it writes for me sound like me.

How to run this:
- Be warm, plain, and quick, like a friend who is good with words.
- Ask me the questions below a few at a time, one round per message, numbered. Keep your messages short.
- After each round, reflect back one or two things you noticed in one sentence, then ask the next round.
- If my answer is thin, offer two or three example answers I can pick from or edit.
- Never invent facts about me or my business.
- I will leave out customers' and family members' names and personal details. If I paste something that includes them anyway, leave those details out of the card and tell me you did.

Round 1: me and my people
1. What do you do, in a sentence or two, the way you would say it to a friend?
2. Who are you writing for? Describe the people you most want to reach.
3. When someone finishes reading something you wrote, how do you want them to feel?

Round 2: how I sound
4. Pick three words for how you want to sound, and one word for how you never want to sound.
5. What words or phrases do you use a lot? These can be your own or your field's.
6. What words, phrases, or styles do you avoid, or make you cringe when you see them?

Round 3: my own writing
7. Paste a short piece of your own writing that you like: an email, a post, or part of a web page, about 100 to 300 words. Remove any customer or family names first.
8. Share a sentence or two you love, by you or anyone, that sounds like the voice you want.

Round 4: my rules
9. What does your writing never do? For example: no exclamation points, no slang, no hard selling, no jargon.
10. Are there topics to keep out of your writing?
11. Any formatting habits? Short paragraphs, lists or no lists, emoji or none, how you sign off.
12. Where will you use AI drafts most: emails, social posts, your website, newsletters, replies to inquiries, or something else?

When all four rounds are done, write my voice card as one page of plain text I can copy anywhere. Use only what I told you and what my writing sample shows, and leave out any line you would have to guess. Use this layout:

VOICE CARD: [my name or business name]
Who I am: [one or two sentences, in my words]
Who I'm writing for: [my people, in my words]
How I want readers to feel: [my answer]
How I sound: [three words], never [one word]
- [three to five short notes on tone, drawn from my answers and my sample]
Words I use: [list]
Words I avoid: [list]
Sentences that sound like me:
1. [a new sentence in my voice, about my work]
2. [a new sentence in my voice, for my people]
3. [a new sentence in my voice, as an opening line]
My writing never:
- [my rules, one per line]
Keep out: [topics] and any customer or family personal details.
Formatting: [my habits]
How to use this card: Paste it at the start of a chat, or into your assistant's custom instructions or project instructions. Then ask for a draft, for example "Using my voice card, write a reply to this inquiry." Read every draft before it goes out, and tell the assistant what to change.

Write the three example sentences fresh, about my real work as I described it. Do not copy my sample word for word, and do not add claims, numbers, results, or credentials I did not give you.

Then ask me: "Does this sound like you? Tell me anything to change, add, or remove." Refine it until I'm happy, and offer to write one short first draft with it.
