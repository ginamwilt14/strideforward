Problem Clarity Worksheet

A free problem statement template and worksheet. One page to separate the symptom from the problem and write it in one sentence you can act on, before you spend money or months on a fix.

Inside the worksheet

  • Write the problem the way you would say it today
  • Sort what you know from what you assume
  • Look at it from the side of the people it affects
  • Rewrite it in one sentence you could act on
  • Choose one small test for the next two weeks

Twenty minutes, on your own or with one colleague. It pairs with the article Solving the wrong problem.

Two fields and you are done. The rest are optional.

The one-sentence template

A problem statement template
you can fill in today.

Step 5 of the worksheet turns your notes into one sentence. Fill in each part. Any part you cannot fill is your next question, and step 6 gives you two weeks to answer it.

[Who] cannot [what they are trying to do] because [the cause you can change], which costs [what it costs them, and you].

Small business example

Before: “Our workshop sign-ups are low. We need more marketing.”

After: “Busy owners who find us on Instagram cannot tell which workshop fits them, so they leave without registering, which costs us people who were ready to come.”

Two-week test: Add three short “this is for you if” lines to the page and compare registrations.

Nonprofit example

Before: “Volunteers keep dropping out. We need a better recruiting campaign.”

After: “New volunteers cannot picture their first shift because sign-up ends with a form and no follow-up, which costs us people who wanted to help and hours of our coordinator’s time.”

Two-week test: Send every new sign-up a short note within a day that says what the first shift involves, and compare how many show up.

Both examples are illustrative. The reframing steps draw on Thomas Wedell-Wedellsborg, “Are You Solving the Right Problems?” Harvard Business Review, January–February 2017. For the longer version, read Solving the wrong problem.
