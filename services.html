How we work

A defined project,
or a partner inside your team.

Every engagement starts the same way: a thirty-minute call with Gina, then a one-page scope note that sets the work, the timeline, and the fee before you commit.

01 · Project

One defined piece of work, start to finish.

Who it is for
Teams with a clear need and a date that matters: a launch, a board meeting, a grant deadline.
How it works
A fixed scope, a timeline, and a fixed fee, all set in the scope note. Most projects run four to eight weeks.
What you leave with
The finished work and the materials to run it, in your hands.
See an example scope note ↗

02 · Fractional (embedded)

A senior partner inside your team, a few hours a week.

Who it is for
Owners and leaders who need ongoing help with growth, programs, or operations before they are ready to hire for it.
How it works
Agreed monthly priorities, set hours inside your team, regular check-ins, and a review every quarter, for a monthly fee set by scope.
What you leave with
Work that moves every week, plus the systems and materials to keep it moving.

Every service below includes hands-on help. We think it through with you, build the pieces, and hand them over working.

01 · Market research and positioning

Know who you serve before you spend.

Before you launch, reprice, or go after a new audience, we find out who buys, who uses, and who funds, what each one needs, and where you fit.

The work can include

  • Customer and stakeholder interviews
  • Market scans and landscape reviews
  • Buyer, user, and funder mapping
  • Offer positioning and messaging

What you leave withA clear picture of your market, a position you can explain in one sentence, and the decisions it points to.

Read: Know Who You Serve Before You Spend ↗

02 · Program development

Programs built for the people they serve.

Turn expertise, a grant deliverable, or an early idea into a program people can use. We design the structure, build the working pieces, and test it with a small first group.

The work can include

  • Program and curriculum design
  • Learning pathways
  • Facilitator and participant materials
  • Pilot design and revision

What you leave withA usable program structure, delivery materials, and a plan to test and improve.

03 · New revenue services and products

Turn what you know into something people pay for.

When the current model no longer covers the work, or you see an opening, we shape a new service, product, or revenue line and test it with real customers before you commit.

The work can include

  • Offer and package design
  • Pricing and business-model design
  • Revenue and cost estimates
  • A small market test

What you leave withA defined offer, the numbers behind it, and a test plan with real customers.

04 · Customer experience design

Map the experience before you fix it.

When customers, families, or donors drop off between first contact and follow-through, we map each step from their side, find the friction, and redesign the moments that matter most.

The work can include

  • Customer experience maps and service blueprints
  • Listening sessions with customers and staff
  • Onboarding and follow-up design
  • Scripts, templates, and handoffs

What you leave withA blueprint of the experience, the fixes in order, and the materials to make them.

Read: Map It Before You Fix It ↗

05 · Business development and sales

Partnerships and sales with a reason to move forward.

We find the organizations and buyers that fit, shape an offer they can say yes to, and help run the outreach, from the first email to the proposal. As a fractional partner, we can carry the pipeline with you week to week.

The work can include

  • Partner and prospect research
  • Outreach and follow-up sequences
  • Proposals and pitch materials
  • Pipeline setup and a weekly pipeline review

What you leave withA prioritized pipeline, the materials to work it, and a sales rhythm your team can keep.

06 · Marketing and sales operations

Systems that follow up for you.

Small teams lose leads in the gaps between a form, an inbox, and a calendar. We set up and connect the tools you already use, so inquiries get answered, calls get booked, and follow-up happens on time.

The work can include

  • CRM setup and clean-up
  • Booking, forms, and email follow-up sequences
  • Automations between your tools
  • A simple view of what is working

What you leave withWorking systems, documented so your team can run and change them.

Common questions

Questions about scope, timing, and fit.

How engagements are shaped, and what to expect before anything is signed.

What is the difference between a project and a fractional partnership?

A project is one defined piece of work with agreed deliverables, a timeline, and a fixed fee. A fractional partnership puts us inside your team for set hours each month on agreed priorities, for a monthly fee, reviewed every quarter. Design and pilot sits in between: build a program or offer, test it with a first group, then refine it.

How much of my time will a project take?

We name it up front. The scope note lists what we need from you: usually a few check-ins, access to the people who know the work, and one person who makes the final calls. We do the research, drafting, and building in between.

What do we own when the work ends?

The deliverables named in your scope note: the materials, templates, plans, and systems we build, set up in your accounts and documented so your team can run and change them. Your agreement spells this out.

What if we do not have funding yet?

Tell us on the call. Sometimes the right first step is the funding case itself, such as a pilot plan or a proposal a funder can review. If the timing is not right, you still leave the call with a next step and a free tool you can use on your own.

Is there something I can share with my board or funder?

Yes. The capabilities overview is a twelve-page PDF covering who we work with, the three kinds of help, three project snapshots, and typical engagements. Request it here; it opens right away.

What do we need to have ready before we talk?

Nothing formal. A description of what you are trying to make possible, and what is getting in the way, is enough to start.

How long does a project take?

It depends on scope. We agree on a timeline before the work begins, and a design and pilot engagement includes time to test with a first group before a wider rollout.

Do you work with nonprofits and community organizations?

Yes. Nonprofits and community organizations use all six services, most often program development, partner and funder outreach, and customer experience work for participants, families, and donors.

Can you help if we have never hired a consultant?

Yes. Many clients have not. We keep the scope small and specific at first, name who does what, and put decisions and materials in your hands as we go.
