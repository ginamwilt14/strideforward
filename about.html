Meet the founder

Hi, I’m Gina.

Most of my career has lived at the intersection of education, leadership, and program design. I’ve helped develop new programs and initiatives, shape strategy for growing organizations, and design learning experiences for the people doing the work.

StrideForward brings that experience to entrepreneurs, small businesses, nonprofits, and learning ventures that need more than advice, but don’t need to build a whole new team.

My doctorate in education and MBA bring together two sides of the work: understanding how people learn and grow, and making the operating model practical and sustainable.

I care about doing careful work, being clear about what the evidence supports, and leaving you with something useful. We start with your context, work within your constraints, and build from there.

StrideForward is the consulting identity of GMW Group LLC. The name is new; the practice and relationships continue.

Why StrideForward exists

Good ideas stall
for two reasons.

Across education, nonprofit, and small-business work, I kept seeing the same pattern. Capable people with a real audience and a good idea would stall, not for lack of effort, but because the problem underneath had never been named, or because no one had the time to turn the plan into working materials.

StrideForward exists to do both: find the real problem with you, then help build the fix, at a pace and scale that fits a small team.

Work like yours

Situation, what we built, what changed. Organizations are described rather than named unless they have said yes to being named.

A college of education at a large public university

Making a workforce portfolio legible to outside buyers.

Situation
Learning, credentials, consulting, research, and events had grown up separately. Districts, employers, and partners could not see how the pieces fit or where to start.
What we built
A connected offering and capability strategy: audience-specific offers, pathways between them, and the market intelligence and financial logic behind each.
What changed
One portfolio that external partners could read, with clear entry points and a shared language across teams.

HIKI Education · Hawaiʻi

Positioning place-based professional learning for schools.

Situation
Strong, culturally grounded programs and a fishpond site, but no clear way for principals and district leaders to understand or contract the professional learning.
What we built
A school-buyer strategy, a soft-launch website, professional-learning packages, a scoping-call-to-proposal path, an operating playbook, and pitch materials for funders and partners.
What changed
School leaders can now see what is offered, who it is for, and how to begin. The team has a playbook to run it.

An early-learning media startup

Building the evidence base and partnership plan for a new product.

Situation
A founder with a product for four- to six-year-olds and strong instincts, but no structured evidence or partnership approach to bring to schools and families.
What we built
A research brief connecting the product to learning science, a pilot plan across parents, homeschool, and classroom settings, and a partnership development approach.
What changed
Product decisions now have a Now, Next, Later logic tied to evidence, and partner conversations start from a clear story.
